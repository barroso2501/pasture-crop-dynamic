# Targeted window sensitivity, entry semantics and native assurance

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** prespecified proposed follow-up; not executed. No new numerical sensitivity result is claimed.

## 1. Rebase the observation boundary

Use the accepted coverage-only categorical engine and aligned fixed domain. Parameterize its initial year as b∈{1985,1995,2000}. At b, observed PAS is baseline-continuous; valid non-PAS is outside PAS; uncertain coverage is uncertain. For b≤year≤t0 apply the same continuation, confirmed non-PAS reset and unresolved-history rules. Attribute endpoint flow PAS(t0)→TMP(t1) at t0. Only the starting year changes; no gap bridging, source-age decoding, native transform change, pixel-domain change or RQ1 cohort change is authorized.

Export the same total flow and three origin areas per cell, b and interval. Validate 24,889 unique identical cell IDs, hashes, nonnegative finite areas, exclusive and exhaustive PAS attribution, origin closure, unchanged total PAS→TMP at each calendar interval, diagnostic flags and equality with accepted 1985 results. Use a new versioned output namespace; never replace canonical b=1985 data.

Make two complementary comparisons. (a) Calendar-matched comparison: common intervals 2000–2005 through 2015–2020, with 2020–2025 separate, comparing different origin definitions on the same endpoint conversions. (b) Follow-up-matched comparison: first four five-year intervals after each b, all ending by 2020, comparing elapsed years since baseline. Record total PAS stock at each b, total flow and cell distribution as well as shares; different b values select different baseline populations. Compare pooled quantities over a common 20-year horizon and over common calendar intervals separately; never compare unequal horizons without reporting them.

The first bar must be 100% baseline-continuous for every b, by construction, and must be annotated. Report descriptive changes in shares and window-specific 50% crossings without treating them as thresholds. Do not declare rebased curves proof of stationarity or quantify an 'artifact fraction': rebasing changes cohort composition and calendar time as well as follow-up. Separating those effects requires a specified renewal/survival null model and conversion hazards or another explicit identification design. A stationary synthetic renewal example can illustrate the mechanism but cannot estimate its contribution to these data.

## 2. First observed entry versus observed re-entry

Keep the existing three-way origin estimator unchanged and add an auxiliary classification attached at the current confirmed spell's entry, carried forward throughout that spell. Track PAS observed strictly before that entry from 1985 onward. `ever_pas_before_entry=true` means confirmed observed re-entry, including a pixel from the fixed RQ1 cohort. If false and all earlier coverage is valid, classify as first observed entry in the window. If no prior PAS is observed but earlier coverage has gaps, classify as prior-history uncertain. Never call even a complete-window first observed entry first-ever establishment: pre-1985 history is unobserved.

The count alternative must be named `observed_confirmed_entry_count` or an observed-spell lower bound; gaps prevent a complete `previous_pas_spell_count`. Freeze the auxiliary flag when entry occurs, not anew at every later PAS year; otherwise a continued first spell would be mislabeled re-entry. Retain uncertainty and boundary-adjacent entry flags.

For PAS→TMP at t0, export the confirmed-entry area divided into observed re-entry, first observed entry with complete earlier history, and uncertain earlier history. Their sum must reproduce the accepted post-1985-entry component per cell and interval; the original three components and total flow must stay unchanged. Test baseline exit/re-entry, repeated re-entry, entry after a confirmed non-PAS reset following a gap, and a continued first spell. Pixel chronology is needed: the existing aggregated CSVs cannot recover this decomposition.
