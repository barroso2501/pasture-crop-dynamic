# Phase 8 synthesis — MAUP robustness

## Scope

Phase 8 tested whether the principal results depend materially on spatial
scale or grid zoning. The canonical approximately 20,000-ha lattice remains
primary throughout. Alternative grids are sensitivity tests constructed over
the same fixed study footprint.

## Evidence by component

| Component | Evidence | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| 8A — alternative supports | 10,000-ha, shifted 20,000-ha and 40,000-ha grids validated | Alternative geometry and fixed support are reproducible |
| 8B — metric reconstruction | 809,800 records; all support and total checks pass | Core process metrics can be recomputed consistently |
| 8C — totals and distributions | 192 aggregate and 48 temporal comparisons | Aggregate signs, dominance and temporal extrema are robust; some conditional distributions are scale-sensitive |
| 8D — Global Moran | 120 alternative tests, all positive and BY-significant | Existence and direction of global clustering are robust; exact magnitude/order partly sensitive |
| 8E — HH clusters | 120 maps; 21/30 overall assessments pass | Local location/persistence is metric-dependent; `NAT → TMP` is sensitive |

## Integrated interpretation

The main aggregate conclusions are not artifacts of the canonical grid. Total
process magnitudes, signs of the C–R balance, dominance classes, peak and trough
intervals, and the positive direction of global spatial autocorrelation are
preserved under the alternative supports.

MAUP sensitivity emerges more clearly as the analysis becomes local and
conditional. Cell-distribution support changes at 40,000 ha, exact Global
Moran magnitudes and temporal rankings vary for some metrics, and HH footprint
location and extent are not uniformly stable.

The sensitivity is strongly metric-dependent:

- replenishment and both C–R balance measures have robust HH location and
  persistence;
- consolidation retains persistent structure but has scale-sensitive
  interval footprints;
- endpoint `NAT → TMP` has robust positive global clustering but sensitive
  local hotspot extent and persistence.

These statements are not contradictory. Aggregates, global autocorrelation
and local cluster membership answer different questions and need not have the
same robustness profile.

## Diagnostic extension

The 2020–2025 interval is included in every full-observed comparison and
flagged as diagnostic. It does not create the central `NAT → TMP`
sensitivity, which is already present in the primary 1985–2020 period. It does
affect some full-window assessments, including 40,000-ha consolidation.

## Final Phase 8 decision

Phase 8 is complete and accepted. Acceptance means that the prespecified
spatial sensitivity design was implemented, validated and reported without
discarding failed substantive criteria. It does not mean spatial results are
invariant to aggregation.

The canonical approximately 20,000-ha lattice remains the primary analytical
support. Manuscript conclusions should distinguish:

1. robust aggregate and global findings;
2. robust local findings for replenishment and C–R balance;
3. partial local robustness for consolidation; and
4. explicit local MAUP sensitivity for endpoint `NAT → TMP`.

Phase 9 can now synthesize temporal, biome and spatial evidence with these
qualifications preserved.
